Ranking Content Refresh Opportunities from Historical Search Signals

A time-aware machine-learning decision-support analysis for prioritizing pages for content review or refresh.

Abdullah Idrees · ML-11 Capstone

Abstract Question Data Methodology Results Limitations Recommendations Reproducibility Data Credit
Abstract

This study asks whether historical search-performance signals can help rank pages for content review. Using anonymized FlyRank warehouse data, I aggregated page-level search and engagement signals and trained a machine-learning model to identify pages associated with positive next-month click outcomes. The model achieved a ROC-AUC of 0.801 and 36.2% precision on the held-out period, compared with a majority-class baseline accuracy of 0.891. The results suggest that impressions and organic sessions were the strongest measured signals for the model's ranking decisions. The resulting scores are intended as directional decision-support recommendations for content review, not as causal evidence that refreshing a page will improve future performance.

1 · Question

Research Question

Can historical search-performance signals be used to rank pages that are the strongest candidates for content refresh or review?

The goal is to help a content team decide which pages should be reviewed first by producing a ranked refresh-opportunity score based on measurable search-performance signals.

Decision supported: This is a decision-support analysis. It does not attempt to explain Google's ranking algorithm and does not establish causal effects of content changes.
2 · Data

Dataset & Data Preparation

This analysis uses the approved FlyRank internship warehouse release available through Hugging Face. The analysis focuses on anonymized search-performance data and aggregates observations at the page and month level.

Public-safety exclusions

Client-identifying information, domains, URLs, private search queries, credentials, and raw private exports are excluded from the public analysis.

Daily performance observations were aggregated into monthly page-level features. Rows without an available future month were removed because they could not receive the next-month outcome label.

3 · Methodology

Methodology

Features

The model uses nine historical page-level signals:

  • Impressions
  • Clicks
  • Average position
  • Pageviews
  • Sessions
  • Engaged sessions
  • Engagement seconds
  • Organic sessions
  • AI sessions

Label definition

A positive outcome is defined when a page receives more clicks in the following month than in the current month.

Positive outcome: next-month clicks > current-month clicks

Model

The final model is a HistGradientBoostingClassifier trained using the historical features above.

Baseline

The baseline always predicts the majority class. On the held-out test period, this baseline achieved 0.891 accuracy.

Validation design

A time-based split was used to avoid mixing future observations into training data.

Split Period
Training 2025-01 through 2026-02
Held-out test 2026-03 through 2026-05

Leakage checks

The future outcome fields next_month_clicks and positive_next_month were not included as model features.

Features were constructed from the current month before the next-month outcome was evaluated.

Interpretation: The model achieved useful ranking/discrimination signal (ROC-AUC = 0.801) and identified a subset of positive-outcome pages with 36.2% precision. However, overall accuracy was below the majority-class baseline. The model should therefore be interpreted as a ranking and decision-support tool rather than an accuracy-optimized classifier.

5 · Limitations

Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The model uses historical aggregated search and engagement signals, so the results describe observed associations rather than causal effects.
  • The evaluation covers a limited future window and may not represent performance in other periods.
  • The positive outcome is based on next-month clicks, so the model is designed for prioritization rather than guaranteed prediction of future success.
  • Some pages have missing GSC or GA4 data, limiting interpretation of engagement-related features.
  • The analysis does not include content quality, topic, backlinks, or other factors that may influence search performance.
The recommendations should therefore be treated as directional, decision-support guidance for content review.
6 · Ranked Recommendations

Action Playbook

The model produces an opportunity score for each page in the held-out evaluation set. Higher scores indicate pages that the model considers stronger candidates for review based on their observed historical signals.

  1. Prioritize high opportunity scores. Use the ranking as a manual review queue rather than treating the score as a guaranteed refresh opportunity.
  2. Investigate high impressions with few clicks. Review title, snippet, and search-intent alignment for pages receiving visibility but relatively few clicks.
  3. Protect strong organic performance. Pages with strong organic sessions should be reviewed carefully before changes are made so existing performance is not damaged.
  4. Investigate good position but low clicks. These pages may deserve a review of click-through opportunity and search-result presentation.
  5. Verify missing engagement data. Before acting on a recommendation, confirm whether GSC or GA4 data is actually available for the page.
The ranking is directional and intended for decision support, not as a causal recommendation that refreshing a page will improve future performance.
